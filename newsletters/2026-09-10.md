## THE BIG STORY
OpenAI's recent announcement of an AI-generated solution to the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem has ignited a firestorm of controversy among academics, casting a shadow over the potential scientific breakthrough. The generative AI lab released a formal proof in Lean, but the claim is overshadowed by accusations of impropriety regarding data usage and transparency in its research process. This incident follows previous concerns raised by mathematicians about whether OpenAI's models accessed unpublished research through user conversations.

Academics, including mathematician Andreas Thom, questioned if their private discussions with **ChatGPT** on related topics could have influenced the AI's "solving process" or training data. While OpenAI categorically denied direct access to specific user data for the specific discovery, it notably stated it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models." This statement exacerbates existing mistrust, particularly within research communities where sharing unreleased work with AI tools now carries new, undefined risks.

The implications extend far beyond this specific mathematical problem, affecting the entire scientific community's confidence in collaborating with AI platforms and their developers. Researchers fear that AI labs might leverage confidential or pre-publication discussions to accelerate their own discoveries without proper attribution or ethical safeguards. This incident spotlights an urgent need for greater transparency from frontier AI developers regarding data provenance and model training methodologies, especially as AI increasingly contributes to fundamental scientific advancements.

## MORE SIGNALS

### Apple's AI Everywhere Strategy
Apple unveiled a suite of new products and features heavily integrated with **Apple Intelligence**. Highlights include the highly anticipated **foldable iPhone Duo**, whose hinge design was developed using AI and 3D printing, and new **Apple Watch** features that transcribe recent speech and summarize ambient conversations. The company also revamped its **Health** app, which now calculates a "health age" and readiness score using AI, and introduced **Apple Reference Image** to verify the authenticity of photos against AI-generated alterations. These features mark a significant step in embedding AI into daily life, simultaneously normalizing always-listening technology while addressing concerns about AI-generated media.

### AI Safety Takes Center Stage
Concerns over AI's potential societal impact escalated this week, fueled by the resignation of Jacob Coxon, a prominent researcher from **Anthropic**. Coxon warned that the people building AI "earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade," calling it "crunch time for humanity." This follows a series of public warnings from other AI safety experts. In related news, **Paul Christiano**, an influential AI researcher focused on alignment, joined the **OpenAI Foundation** board and its Safety and Security Committee, signaling a concerted effort to address these critical issues at the highest levels of leading AI organizations.

### The Rise of Intelligent Agents
The "agentic Gemini era" is officially underway, as Google I/O 2026 highlighted how new AI agents are expanding capabilities. Simultaneously, reports indicate that AI agents are already "flooding public services with new requests," many for legitimate claims, signaling the technology's growing real-world impact. Google further bolstered its agent offerings with the launch of the **Gemini app for Windows** and **Lyria 3.5** for music generation. Meta also introduced **Muse**, a new personal AI agent designed to compete with rivals by handling tasks from selling cars to booking flights, emphasizing built-in privacy. Cognition also released **SWE-2**, its advanced coding model, rivaling other top AI solutions.
