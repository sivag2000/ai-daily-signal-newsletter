# The Agentic Shift
**Issue #050 · July 15, 2026**
*AI is no longer just answering prompts; it's taking action, shaping enterprise and consumer experiences.*

## TL;DR
-   OpenAI introduces **ChatGPT Work**, an agentic AI that takes actions across apps, marking a shift to active AI systems.
-   **Anthropic-backed Ode** launches, betting enterprise AI adoption hinges on embedding forward-deployed engineers, not just models.
-   Google expands **Managed Agents in Gemini API**, empowering developers to build reliable, production-ready AI agents for diverse applications.

## THE BIG STORY
The AI landscape is rapidly evolving beyond passive large language models to proactive, agentic systems capable of independent action across applications and workflows. OpenAI’s launch of **ChatGPT Work** signals a significant leap in this direction, positioning the AI as an agent that can execute multi-step tasks across apps and files, persisting for hours to achieve a user-defined goal. This move is complemented by the deployment of **GPT-5.6** as the preferred model in Microsoft 365 Copilot, promising stronger AI capabilities for faster, higher-quality work across essential business tools like Word and Excel.

This shift marks a critical transition for enterprises and developers. Instead of merely generating text or code, AI is now designed to integrate deeply into operational processes, performing useful work per dollar and scaling high-value workflows, according to OpenAI's guidance on managing AI investments. Google is also embracing this agentic era, introducing new capabilities in **Managed Agents in the Gemini API** and the **Interactions API** to provide developers with a unified interface for building reliable, production-ready agents. This concerted industry push toward autonomous AI agents will profoundly impact how businesses automate tasks, manage information, and interact with technology, demanding new strategies for oversight and integration.

The implications are far-reaching, affecting everyone from business leaders seeking efficiency gains to developers building next-generation applications. While promising unprecedented productivity, this agentic shift also introduces complexities around data governance, security, and the reliability of autonomous systems, underscoring the need for robust evaluation and management frameworks. Companies like Rime, which recently secured **$24 million** in Series A funding for its AI that handles over **100 million customer calls monthly**, exemplify the immediate value proposition of AI agents in specific verticals.

## MORE SIGNALS
### Implementation, Not Models, Drives Value
AI labs are increasingly looking beyond foundational model development for the next trillion-dollar opportunity. Anthropic, backed by Blackstone, is supporting **Ode**, a new venture focused on embedding forward-deployed engineers directly within enterprises. This strategy bets that the key to accelerating AI adoption and unlocking significant value lies in expert implementation and integration, rather than solely on the continuous advancement of core AI models.

### India's Emergent Reaches Unicorn Status
Indian AI coding startup **Emergent** has achieved unicorn valuation with a **$130 million Series C** funding round, just over a year after launch. The company boasts a **$120 million annualized revenue run rate** and serves more than **200,000 paying customers**, highlighting robust demand for AI-powered coding tools and the rapid growth potential in the global AI market.

### Standardizing AI Agent Identification
Internet pioneer Vint Cerf is working on a plan to establish a standard for identifying AI agents operating on the open internet. Recognizing the increasing prevalence of autonomous AI systems, this initiative aims to create a framework—similar to **TCP/IP** for the internet itself—that allows for clear identification and differentiation of AI agents from human users or traditional software, addressing concerns around transparency and accountability.

## WHAT TO WATCH
The expansion of agentic AI systems demands closer scrutiny on ethical guardrails and accountability frameworks. As AI moves from assistants to active participants, the industry will need to navigate challenges like "hallucinating" agents and the difficulty of tracking autonomous actions, alongside potential for new forms of AI fraud, such as sophisticated voice deepfakes.
