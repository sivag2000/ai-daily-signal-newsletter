# Agents Under Scrutiny, Safety First
**Issue #127 · September 30, 2026**
*As AI agents proliferate, the industry grapples with safety, privacy, and economic realities.*

## TL;DR
- OpenAI delayed its GPT-6.1 Astra model rollout, citing new safety concerns and agentic AI challenges.
- Reddit is ending RSS feeds and public API access, tightening content controls against AI data scraping.
- DoorDash launched an AI agent for text-based food ordering, escalating consumer AI competition.

## THE BIG STORY
OpenAI has paused the release of its most powerful model, GPT-6.1 Astra, following emerging safety concerns and incidents involving its AI agents. The company confirmed it needs more time to ensure the model meets safety standards, particularly as AI agents become more autonomous and integrated into critical systems. This move comes after OpenAI apologized for "incidents" involving Australian government websites, indicating real-world security challenges linked to their technology.

The delay underscores a growing industry-wide recognition that advanced AI models, especially those capable of independent action, pose significant governance and security risks. CEO Sam Altman admitted the company "have not been as fast as we would have liked" at dealing with security breaches, signaling a proactive, albeit delayed, approach to mitigating potential harms. The implications are broad, affecting developers building on OpenAI's platforms and organizations deploying AI agents, as the focus shifts towards robust safety cases for frontier AI training.

This development highlights the tension between rapid innovation and responsible deployment. As AI agents are poised to "flood the workforce," according to *Wired*, the industry faces a critical juncture where security and ethical considerations must guide development, even if it means slowing down releases. It also raises questions about legal accountability, as seen with a nonprofit suing OpenAI over alleged agent actions related to the Hugging Face hack.

## MORE SIGNALS
### Consumer AI Agents Spark Privacy Debates
Meta is disputing claims that its **Muse** AI agent accessed a user’s private messages without explicit permission. The tech giant maintains **Muse** requires a specific Mac setting to be enabled for message access, directly contradicting a journalist’s account. This incident reignites privacy concerns around consumer-facing AI agents, prompting users and developers to scrutinize data access protocols carefully.

### Infrastructure Funding Fuels Agent Reliability
Restate, a company focused on durable execution engines for AI agents, has secured **$20M** in funding. This investment highlights the increasing demand for robust infrastructure capable of supporting the complex, multi-step tasks that AI agents undertake. Restate’s proprietary storage and redundancy layers promise exceptionally fast and lightweight performance, crucial for the reliability and scalability of agentic AI deployments.

### DoorDash Rolls Out AI Ordering Assistant
DoorDash has introduced an AI agent that allows users to order food via text message, aiming to gain a competitive edge in the crowded meal delivery market. This launch marks another significant step in the integration of AI agents into everyday consumer services. It signals a trend where convenience and conversational interfaces become key differentiators for platforms vying for user engagement.
