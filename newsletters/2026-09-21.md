## THE BIG STORY
Meta's new AI agent, **Muse**, has been explicitly blocked from operating on Amazon.com, according to TechCrunch. This move by Amazon highlights growing tensions between major tech companies as AI agents begin to interact directly with existing digital ecosystems, raising questions about data access, user experience, and competitive advantage. The immediate consequence is that Muse users cannot leverage their AI for shopping on the dominant e-commerce platform.

This blocking underscores a critical emerging challenge for the proliferation of AI agents: interoperability and platform control. As AI agents like Muse aim to simplify complex tasks across various online services, their success hinges on seamless integration—a goal that platform owners like Amazon can easily disrupt. This friction could lead to a fragmented AI agent landscape, where different agents are restricted to specific, approved domains, or where companies must negotiate access agreements, rather than assuming open interaction.

The move primarily impacts Meta, limiting the utility of its agent in a key consumer activity, and by extension, users who expected a unified AI shopping experience. It also serves as a precedent for other platforms to control how external AI agents can interact with their services, affecting developers and businesses building agents that rely on broad web access. The larger question looms: will the future of AI agents be one of open interaction or controlled ecosystems?

## MORE SIGNALS
### Google Doubles Down on AI Hardware
Google has launched the **Googlebook**, an **$899** laptop designed from the ground up for its **Gemini** AI. This new device integrates Gemini directly into the cursor, dictation, widgets, and other core desktop experiences. Concurrently, Google also made its Gemini app available for Windows, demonstrating a multi-platform push to embed its AI.

### OpenAI Expands Industry-Specific AI & Safety Protocols
OpenAI announced **Astra for Law**, offering frontier intelligence, custom firm workflows, and legal-grade controls for confidential client work. Alongside this, the company introduced the Australian Youth Safety Blueprint, a six-pillar roadmap for safer AI experiences for young people, and a new framework for tracking, investigating, and disclosing model misalignment after reporting six incidents of unexpected model behavior.

### World Models and AI Vulnerabilities Remain Murky
Despite significant investment and buzz, companies developing "world models" are reportedly keeping their progress under wraps, with little transparency regarding what they are actually building. This secrecy comes as Wired cautions against an "AI slowdown," instead pointing to a "vulnerability explosion" where widely available AI chatbots are already helping uncover a tidal wave of security flaws in existing systems.
